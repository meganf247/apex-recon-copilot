2 · Why this matters to Apex

  • Apex sits in the middle of the money: DSPs pay Apex, Apex pays publishers and keeps its take (14.3% on average). Q3 2026: $432M gross CTV spend, $61.9M Apex net revenue.
  • When a DSP pays on a lower count, who absorbs it depends on the publisher's terms:
    • Publisher paid on Apex's count (most guaranteed deals): Apex still owes the publisher, so Apex absorbs the whole gap.
    • Publisher paid on cleared revenue: the publisher absorbs most of it; Apex loses its take, and the publisher relationship takes the hit.
  • Q3: $24.4M billing shortfall across all deals (5.7% of gross). $18.1M of it is on 715 deal-months over contract tolerance across 39 of 40 DSPs: that's the revenue at risk. $8.9M of that hits Apex's own P&L.
  • Disputed invoices sit unpaid: about $200K of revenue at risk a day.

Timezone shift DSP reports in US time; primetime crosses midnight UTC Ask for UTC reporting; no billing change $0.6M $0.3M

Root causes are the model's calls on the Q3 queue. Recovery rates are Billing Ops' actual Jan–Jun 2026 outcomes. Real cases are often mixed (e.g. a billing error plus some IVT); the model names the main cause.

4 · Today vs. tomorrow

Today: download each DSP's file → spreadsheet lookups → email back and forth → guess the cause → write off what's left.

Tomorrow: 1. Partner files are ingested and checked automatically (Lakeflow). 2. Every deal is reconciled against Apex's logs, daily and monthly. 3. A model explains why each gap happened, and a draft dispute is ready (AI). 4. Analysts work one ranked queue and resolve in a click (Databricks App + Lakebase). 5. Finance asks questions in plain English (Genie).
